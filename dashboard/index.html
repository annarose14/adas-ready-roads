ADAS-Ready Roads

Can a camera-based lane-keeping system see painted lane lines on Sydney's roads? An agent screens crowd-sourced street imagery with OpenCV 5, asks a vision-language model on AWS when it needs a second look, checks OpenStreetMap, and routes uncertain places to people.

Research prototype. Verdicts are screening hints to prioritise human inspection, not safety certifications. See "Validated performance" for what has and has not been demonstrated.

Validated performance (blind human labels)

  • Clean test (4 never-used suburbs, 56 locations): where painted lines were visible, the system confirmed them in 42 of 48 decided locations (88%, 95% CI 75–94%) and routed 7 to human review.
  • Missing markings: not yet demonstrated. Roads without lines are rare in crowd imagery (16 of 133 labelled locations); the system found 9 of those 16 (56%, very uncertain).
  • OpenCV-only versions scored at chance on unseen suburbs; OpenStreetMap alone was a poor guide (54%). Human self-agreement on paint wear was poor (kappa 0.14), so wear is not graded.

    Try it live (AWS)

    One photo: OpenCV 5 checks it is a usable road photo, then a vision-language model on Amazon Bedrock is asked whether painted lane lines are visible. The first request can take ~15 s while the server starts.
